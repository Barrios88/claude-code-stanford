About John Barrios

John Barrios is an Associate Professor of Accounting at the Yale School of Management and a Faculty Research Fellow at the National Bureau of Economic Research, in the Political Economy and the Productivity, Innovation, and Entrepreneurship groups.

His research looks at how accounting information and the rules that govern it shape firms and the people who run them. He studies who enters the accounting profession, who starts a business, how politics affects the way people read and act on the numbers, and how disclosure regulation interacts with antitrust enforcement. His work has been published in the Journal of Accounting Research, the Journal of Public Economics, American Economic Journal: Microeconomics, Management Science and the Journal of Financial Economics.

He is Editor of Accounting Horizons, an Ad Hoc Advising Editor at the Journal of Accounting and Economics, and Guest Editor of the Journal of Labor Economics Symposium on Occupational Licensing. He co-founded the Labor and Accounting Group.

He also works on how researchers can use AI agents without giving up verification or ownership of their results. He teaches a PhD course on Claude Code for accounting research, of which this session is a short version, and maintains an open-source set of agent skills for empirical research, including a WRDS connector.

Before Yale, he taught at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business and at the Olin Business School at Washington University in St. Louis. He holds a PhD in Business Administration and an MPA in Accounting from the University of Miami, and a BS in Industrial and Labor Relations from Cornell University.
