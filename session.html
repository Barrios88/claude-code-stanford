Block by block

Framing (20 minutes)

A chat window answers questions. An agent like Claude Code works in a folder on your machine: it reads your files, writes scripts, runs them, looks at the output and tries again. That makes it more useful for empirical work, and it means you need ways to control it. We cover four: a CLAUDE.md file that holds the project’s standing instructions, the permission prompts that ask before Claude edits a file or runs a command, plan mode (Shift+Tab) for agreeing on a plan before anything runs, and Esc to stop it mid-step. For the rest of the session, we check every number the agent reports against a source it did not produce.

Live demo 1 (25 minutes)

I take one research idea on FSDS data from an empty folder to a finished figure. Watch three things. The opening prompt has six parts (DATA, JOIN, MEASURE, AGGREGATION, OUTPUT, PAPER TRAIL), and you will reuse it in Lab 1. The paper trail gets written as the work happens: DATA_PROFILE.md before any figure, DECISIONS.md for every choice, and LOG.md for every step. And at least one thing goes wrong on purpose, such as a download refused with “403 Forbidden” for a missing User-Agent or duplicate rows that would double-count a firm, so you can see how the agent recovers and where I step in. The demo ends by checking one number against the actual 10-K on EDGAR.

Lab 1 (50 minutes)

You run the same loop yourself on the quarters you downloaded. Pick a tier (cash holdings, R&D intensity, or your own question), adapt lab1_opening_prompt.md, and finish with lab1_verify_checklist.md. Full instructions are on the Lab 1 page.

Demo 2 and Lab 2a (30 minutes)

A short demo applies the same steps to text: pulling the Risk Factors section (Item 1A) from a set of 10-Ks, counting a keyword, caching every download so a rerun fetches nothing twice, and writing a quality report that says which filings failed and why. Then you package your Lab 1 pipeline as a skill, .claude/skills/fsds-panel/SKILL.md, starting from SKILL_skeleton.md, and test that it fires in a fresh session on a request that doesn’t name it. Instructions are in Lab 2, Part A.

Lab 2b: verification drills (25 minutes)

Three short drills from lab2_verification_drills.md, all on your own project: the two-check credential audit, a clean-room referee that sees only your figure caption or paper-trail summary, and one deny rule in .claude/settings.json that you test. Instructions are in Lab 2, Part B.

Discussion and exit ticket (20 minutes)

We discuss three questions, one each on learning, on verification and on the market for papers, using the two readings and your own experience from the labs. The discussion questions for each paper are on the Readings page.

The exit ticket (exit_ticket.md in the starter pack) asks for four short answers:

  • one thing that broke today, and how you found out;
  • one thing you will verify next time before you trust a result;
  • one research task you will delegate to an agent;
  • one research task you won’t.
