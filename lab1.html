Lab 1: From FSDS to a Verified Figure

Download the starter pack (zip) Slides Agenda

In this lab you run the loop from the first demo yourself: raw SEC data in, a figure out, and a written record of every decision in between. You have 50 minutes. The data is already on your laptop from the Before Class page, so nothing in this lab depends on the room’s wifi except Claude itself.

You need three files from ~/stanford_starter:

  • CLAUDE.md.template. Fill in its three blanks and save it as ~/stanford_lab/CLAUDE.md before you start Claude.
  • lab1_opening_prompt.md, the opening prompt with a blank for each tier.
  • lab1_verify_checklist.md, the checklist you finish with.
NoteWhat you will have at the end

When the lab is done, your ~/stanford_lab folder should contain:

  • Scripts that go from the raw FSDS files to your figure. Everything the agent did should be in a file you can rerun.
  • DATA_PROFILE.md, written before you make any figure. It says what one row of each file is, checks that this is true, and shows how often each tag you use appears.
  • DECISIONS.md, listing each choice you made and why.
  • LOG.md, listing each step.
  • A table showing how many observations are left after each filter.
  • One figure.
  • One number from your data that you checked against the firm’s 10-K on EDGAR.

The data

You have three quarters of the SEC’s Financial Statement Data Sets in ~/stanford_lab/fsds/: 2016q1/, 2020q1/ and 2024q1/. FSDS groups filings by the quarter the SEC received them. Most US firms have a December year end and file their 10-K in February or March, so these three files hold mostly fiscal years 2015, 2019 and 2023. Group your results by the fiscal year column fy in sub.txt, not by the name of the folder.

Two files in each folder matter:

  • sub.txt has one row per filing: the accession number (adsh), the firm’s CIK, its SIC industry code, the form type, the fiscal year and the period the filing covers.
  • num.txt has one row per reported value: the accession number again, the XBRL tag (such as Assets), the date the value applies to (ddate), the number of quarters it spans (qtrs), the unit (uom), two fields called segments and coreg, and the value.

You join the two on adsh. Then four filters apply to every tier, and the opening prompt states all of them:

  • Consolidated rows only. Keep rows where segments and coreg are blank and uom is USD. A blank segments field means the value is for the whole firm rather than one business segment, and a blank coreg means it is for the parent filer rather than a co-registrant. About half the rows in a recent num.txt are segment values, so skipping this filter can count the same firm’s number several times.
  • 10-Ks only. Keep form = 10-K.
  • The right qtrs. Use qtrs = 0 for balance-sheet values (stocks, such as Assets or cash) and qtrs = 4 for full-year flows (such as R&D expense).
  • One quarter at a time. Each num.txt is about 400 to 470 MB. Read one quarter, keep only the tags and columns you need, then move to the next, rather than loading all three at once.

A 10-K also reports last year’s numbers next to this year’s, so the same firm-year value can show up in two filings. Keep values whose ddate matches the filing’s own period end, and write that rule into DECISIONS.md.

Choose your tier

Pick one. The tiers differ in how much cleaning judgment they ask of you. Pick the one that fits the time and your interests, not necessarily the hardest.

Base: cash holdings. Cash and cash equivalents divided by total assets, as the median across firms for each fiscal year. The numerator tag is CashAndCashEquivalentsAtCarryingValue and the denominator is Assets, both with qtrs = 0. Check the cash tag’s coverage in DATA_PROFILE.md before you compute anything.

What to expect, from the class files (share of 10-Ks with a consolidated value for the filing’s own period end):

Tag 2016q1 2020q1 2024q1
Assets 98.4% 98.7% 99.4%
CashAndCashEquivalentsAtCarryingValue 91.3% 85.1% 80.5%
CashCashEquivalentsRestrictedCashAndRestrictedCashEquivalents 0% 57.5% 79.3%

The main cash tag loses coverage after the 2018 restricted-cash standard, as filers move to the tag that includes restricted cash. The median cash-to-assets ratio is about 7 to 9 percent, and part of the rise in the last year comes from which firms drop out of the main tag. Report the median both ways: main tag only, and with the restricted-cash tag as a fallback. Financial firms are more than a quarter of filers, so also say whether you keep them.

Standard: R&D intensity by industry. R&D expense divided by total assets: ResearchAndDevelopmentExpense with qtrs = 4 over Assets with qtrs = 0. Report the median by two-digit SIC code (sic2, the first two digits of sic in sub.txt) for the five largest sic2 groups, by fiscal year. You decide what “largest” means (most firms reporting R&D is the simplest choice) and record it in DECISIONS.md.

What to expect: ResearchAndDevelopmentExpense appears in 26 to 33 percent of 10-Ks in these files. By that count the five largest groups are SIC 28 (chemicals and pharmaceuticals), 73 (business services, including software), 38 (instruments), 36 (electronics) and 35 (machinery and computers). SIC 28’s median is far above the rest, above 20 percent of assets, because it holds hundreds of small biotechs.

Check at least one large firm against its 10-K. A tag name can mislead: in Johnson & Johnson’s fiscal 2023 filing, ResearchAndDevelopmentExpense is $483 million, a charge for buying in-process R&D, while its income-statement R&D of about $15.1 billion sits under ResearchAndDevelopmentExpenseExcludingAcquiredInProcessCost.

One warning. Firms with no R&D mostly don’t report the tag at all, so in this data zero R&D shows up as a missing tag, not as a zero. Treat a missing tag as missing, not as zero, and say in DECISIONS.md that your median covers only firms that report R&D.

Stretch: your own question. Use the research question you brought. It has to be answerable, at least in part, from these three quarters of FSDS. Find the tags you need first and check their coverage in DATA_PROFILE.md before you build anything, because a tag that appears in 5 percent of 10-Ks will not support a figure. The stretch tier uses the same opening prompt structure, the same paper trail and the same verify checklist as the other two.

Run it

Work in this order. Each step catches a different kind of mistake before it reaches the figure.

  1. Start Claude in your data folder.

    cd ~/stanford_lab && claude

  2. Fill in the opening prompt. Open lab1_opening_prompt.md. It has six parts: DATA, JOIN, MEASURE, AGGREGATION, OUTPUT and PAPER TRAIL. Fill in the blanks for your tier in MEASURE and AGGREGATION, and change OUTPUT if you want a different figure. Leave the PAPER TRAIL part exactly as written, even if you’re short on time, because it is what produces DATA_PROFILE.md, DECISIONS.md and LOG.md. Paste the prompt and send it.

  3. Read DATA_PROFILE.md as soon as it exists, before any measure is computed. Check the row counts, the uniqueness test (the number of rows should equal the number of distinct keys) and the tag coverage for each quarter. If anything looks wrong, stop and ask about it now. Press Esc if Claude is already moving on.

  4. Look at the raw data. Ask for a plot of the firm-level ratio for each year, before any medians. Extreme values and impossible ratios (cash above total assets, say) are much easier to see here than in a median.

  5. Make the figure. Let Claude draw it, then refine it in no more than three rounds.

  6. Check one number against the 10-K. Pick one firm in your sample with a December year end. These are first-quarter files, so firms with other year ends (Apple’s ends in September) mostly appear only through their 10-Qs. Find its 10-K for one of your fiscal years on EDGAR, open the balance sheet (or the income statement, for R&D), and compare the value with the raw value in your data, before any division. Write the firm, the filing, the two numbers and whether they match into DECISIONS.md.

  7. Work through the checklist below.

WarningCommon failure: a clean-looking figure

A figure with tidy axes and a sensible trend can still be wrong. A missing consolidated-row filter, for example, can inflate values and still produce a smooth line. Work through the checklist even when the figure looks fine.

TipVerify this: the Lab 1 checklist

The same list is in lab1_verify_checklist.md, with space to write in your checks. It is pass/fail and you grade yourself.

If you finish early

Pick one:

  • Do the next tier up, in the same folder, and compare what changed in your decisions.
  • Test your README: copy the folder without the outputs and check that a classmate could rerun the pipeline from it.

Keep this folder. Lab 2 turns this pipeline into a skill.
